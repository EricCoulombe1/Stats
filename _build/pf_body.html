Our own rentals

Our portfolio, by the numbers

My wife and I own rental properties in the same northern BC towns I recommend to clients. Here are two of them with the real figures: rent, costs, mortgage, cash flow and today's assessed value, including the months that didn't go to plan.

In plain English

Both properties pay us every month after every cost and the mortgage. Neither looks like a windfall on assessed value right now. Both are doing the job they were bought for: steady, positive cash flow that doesn't depend on prices rising.

A small, simple complex in a quiet part of town backing onto Crown land: no elevators, parkade or shared boiler to go wrong. Kitimat's rents are driven by the aluminum smelter and the LNG Canada build.

This is the wheel in practice. After a post-COVID jump in values we refinanced and put about C$50,000 of equity to work on our next purchase. The bigger mortgage didn't break it: it still pays us close to C$900 a month.

The assessed value today is below that refinance appraisal. We show it anyway. We hold this one for income, and the income has held.

The kind of property I look for: a newer house with a self-contained suite, so two rents share one roof, one lot and one mortgage. Separate meters and heating, extra soundproofing between floors.

2026 has been a real-world year. One month sat almost empty between tenants, and finding new ones cost C$3,000 in placement fees. Rent collected so far averages C$5,452 a month against a full C$6,000. It still paid us about C$690 a month after everything, and fully rented months have netted C$1,400 to C$1,900.

At a 5% mortgage rate, the cash flow so far would be about C$390 a month: thinner, still positive. The assessed value is about 10% below what we paid. The house wasn't bought to be sold soon, and the coverage tests I use are built for exactly this.

What these two show

Cash flow first, because prices don't move on schedule

Refinancing

The bigger mortgage didn't break it

Kitimat gave back about C$50,000 of equity for the next purchase and still clears close to C$900 a month. That's what the refinance tests are for.

Turnover

Vacancy and re-renting are real costs

A slow month, lower rents between tenants and two placements have cost Fort St. John about C$8,700 so far this year. Budget for it, and keep reserves for it.

Values

Prices move both ways

Both assessments sit below an earlier mark. Neither changes the monthly income, which is why I buy for cash flow and treat appreciation as a bonus.
