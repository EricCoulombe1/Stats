Spinning Wheel:
No refinancing:

The rule of thumb. Each turn of the wheel adds roughly (amount moved ÷ your original cash) × (what the next property earns − what the borrowed money costs). When the properties earn more than the mortgage rate, every turn adds. When they earn less, every turn subtracts. Recycling equity doesn't change what a property earns. It changes how much property each original dollar controls.
How this model works
  • Starting point: one $110,000 contribution, never topped up. That's 20% down on a $500,000 suited home or duplex, plus about 2% in closing costs.
  • Every mortgage: 80% of the price, 30-year amortization, at the rate you set, held constant through renewals.
  • Rent: net operating income starts at the cap rate you choose (Baseline 7.0%) and grows with property values. Strong and Premium represent newer, top-of-range rentals.
  • Refinancing: on the schedule you set, each property is refinanced up to your limit. Each refinance costs $2,000, starts a fresh 30-year amortization, and is skipped if it would free up less than $5,000.
  • Safety rule: no refinance or purchase may push a property's rent below 1.25 times its mortgage payment. At higher rates this limits how much you can borrow, so you need a bigger down payment.
  • Buying: once the cash pool covers 20% down plus closing costs on a home at that year's price, the model buys it. No purchase in the final year.
  • No refinancing comparison: identical rules, but equity is never borrowed against. Reinvested cash flow can still buy homes.
  • Selling: all results assume everything is sold in the final year at 4% selling costs.
  • Not included: income tax, capital gains tax, vacancies beyond normal, major repairs, rate changes at renewal, mortgage penalties, and whether a lender will approve each loan. All figures are nominal and pre-tax.
What could go wrong
  • Rates rise at renewal. Payments climb on every mortgage at once. The model holds your rate constant, so test a higher one.
  • Values stall or fall. The refinance capacity disappears for years, and purchases get pushed back. A falling market delays the wheel; being forced to sell is what breaks it.
  • Lenders tighten. Qualifying for more mortgages gets harder as a portfolio grows, whatever the numbers say.
  • More debt, thinner margins. Each refinance leaves the original property carrying more debt. Keep reserves outside this model.
